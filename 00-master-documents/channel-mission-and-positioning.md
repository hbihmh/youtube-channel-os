# Channel Mission & Positioning

## 1. Exact Channel Niche & Target Audience Profile

### Channel Niche

This channel is a **computer science and artificial intelligence education channel focused on understanding technical concepts from first principles and connecting theory to implementation**.

The channel sits at the intersection of:

- Computer Science fundamentals
- Python programming
- Mathematics for computing and AI
- Machine Learning
- Deep Learning
- Artificial Intelligence
- Software Engineering concepts relevant to AI systems
- Practical implementation and experimentation
- Advanced AI topics and, eventually, research

The channel's central educational approach is:

> **Understand the problem → build the intuition → formalize the concept → understand the mathematics/logic → implement it → experiment with it → understand its limitations.**

The channel is not designed to be a collection of disconnected tutorials. It is intended to become a **structured, long-term educational library** in which individual videos form a coherent curriculum.

### Primary Target Audience

The primary audience is:

**BSCS students and serious self-learners who want to understand Computer Science and AI beyond surface-level tutorials.**

Typical audience characteristics:

- Beginner to intermediate programming ability.
- Learning or using Python.
- Studying Computer Science formally or independently.
- Interested in AI, Machine Learning, and software development.
- Comfortable learning technical material but often struggle to connect mathematical theory with working code.
- Want to understand *why* a system works, not merely how to call a library.
- Prefer structured learning paths over random collections of videos.
- Want knowledge that remains useful beyond a particular framework, library, or AI trend.

### Secondary Audience

The channel may also serve:

- Software developers transitioning toward AI/ML.
- University students studying ML or AI courses.
- Technical professionals strengthening their fundamentals.
- Advanced learners interested in implementations, experiments, and research-oriented material.

### Positioning Statement

> **For BSCS students and serious self-learners who want to understand Computer Science and AI deeply, this channel provides structured explanations that connect concepts, mathematics, logic, implementation, experimentation, and limitations—without reducing difficult subjects to black-box tutorials or hype.**

## 2. The Core Intellectual Promise

The channel's fundamental promise is **depth with implementation**.

A viewer should not leave a major technical lecture knowing only a definition or a code snippet. They should understand the intellectual chain connecting the problem to the solution.

### The Learning Chain

Every major topic should aim to follow this progression:

```text
REAL PROBLEM
     ↓
WHY THE PROBLEM MATTERS
     ↓
INTUITION
     ↓
FORMAL CONCEPT
     ↓
MATHEMATICS / LOGIC
     ↓
ALGORITHM
     ↓
IMPLEMENTATION
     ↓
EXPERIMENT
     ↓
LIMITATIONS & ASSUMPTIONS
     ↓
NEXT CONCEPT
```

### What "Understanding" Means

For important concepts, the channel aims to help viewers answer:

1. **What is this?**
2. **Why does it exist?**
3. **What problem does it solve?**
4. **How does it work conceptually?**
5. **What mathematics or logic supports it?**
6. **How can it be implemented?**
7. **What happens when we change important variables or assumptions?**
8. **Where does it fail or become insufficient?**
9. **What concept should I learn next?**

### Theory-to-Code Principle

The channel should avoid two extremes:

**Theory without implementation**

```text
Definition → Equation → Memorize
```

and

**Implementation without understanding**

```text
Import Library → Call Function → Get Result
```

Instead:

```text
Theory
  ↓
Mathematics / Logic
  ↓
Algorithm
  ↓
Code
  ↓
Experiment
```

Libraries and frameworks may be used, but they should not replace conceptual understanding when the educational objective is to understand the underlying mechanism.

### Limitations Are Part of the Lesson

Every substantial technical topic should distinguish between:

- What the concept guarantees.
- What assumptions it makes.
- Where it works well.
- Where it breaks down.
- What simplified explanations leave out.

The channel does not treat limitations as optional material. Understanding **when an idea fails** is part of understanding the idea itself.

### Intellectual Honesty

The creator does not need to present themselves as an all-knowing expert.

The channel prioritizes:

- Accuracy over confidence.
- Evidence over repetition.
- Understanding over memorization.
- Clear uncertainty over unsupported claims.
- Verified experiments over demonstrations that merely appear convincing.

If something is uncertain, version-dependent, actively researched, or simplified for teaching, that should be communicated clearly.

### Long-Term Educational Promise

The channel is building more than individual videos.

> **The long-term goal is to create a coherent public curriculum that helps a learner progress from foundational Computer Science and Python through Machine Learning, Deep Learning, modern AI systems, and eventually research-oriented topics.**

## 3. The 70/20/10 Content Distribution Rule

The channel uses a **70/20/10 distribution model** to balance long-term educational value with practical relevance and current developments.

### 70% — Evergreen

**Primary purpose:** Build the permanent educational library.

Evergreen content teaches concepts that should remain useful for years regardless of changing tools or trends.

Examples:

- Python fundamentals
- Data structures and algorithms
- Linear algebra for ML
- Probability and statistics
- Calculus and optimization
- Machine Learning fundamentals
- Linear regression
- Logistic regression
- Gradient descent
- Loss functions
- Neural networks
- Backpropagation
- Attention mechanisms
- Transformer fundamentals
- Software engineering principles

**Rule:**

> If the video remains valuable even after today's popular AI tools disappear, it is likely evergreen.

Evergreen content forms the **backbone of the channel**.

---

### 20% — Applied

**Primary purpose:** Turn understanding into practical capability.

Applied content demonstrates how concepts become working systems.

Examples:

- Implementing algorithms from scratch.
- Building ML projects.
- Python-based experiments.
- Model evaluation projects.
- Building AI applications.
- Reproducing an algorithm from a paper.
- Comparing implementations.
- Debugging and analyzing model behavior.
- End-to-end projects.

Applied videos should connect back to the curriculum rather than becoming random project tutorials.

Preferred relationship:

```text
CONCEPT LECTURE
      ↓
PRACTICAL LAB
      ↓
PROJECT
```

For example:

```text
Gradient Descent Explained
        ↓
Gradient Descent From Scratch
        ↓
Linear Regression Project
```

---

### 10% — Current

**Primary purpose:** Maintain relevance without allowing trends to control the channel.

Current content may include:

- Significant AI research developments.
- Major model releases.
- Important technical changes.
- New tools or frameworks when they have genuine educational value.
- Research papers worth analyzing.
- Important developments in the AI ecosystem.

Current content must still satisfy the channel's intellectual standards.

The existence of a trend is **not sufficient reason to make a video**.

A current topic should ideally answer one of these questions:

- Does this reveal an important technical concept?
- Does it demonstrate a meaningful shift in the field?
- Can it be connected to the existing curriculum?
- Will understanding it help viewers interpret future developments?

### Distribution Principle

The 70/20/10 rule is a strategic guideline, not a rigid mathematical requirement.

The priority order is:

```text
LONG-TERM EDUCATIONAL VALUE
        ↓
CURRICULUM COHERENCE
        ↓
TECHNICAL ACCURACY
        ↓
PRACTICAL USEFULNESS
        ↓
CURRENT RELEVANCE
        ↓
ALGORITHM / TREND POTENTIAL
```

The algorithm should influence **distribution and presentation**, not determine the intellectual direction of the channel.

## 4. Content Anti-Objectives

The channel deliberately avoids content strategies that may generate short-term attention at the expense of long-term credibility.

### 4.1 Hype-Driven AI Content

Avoid videos whose primary value is sensationalism.

Examples of titles or approaches to avoid:

- "This AI Just DESTROYED Everything!"
- "The END of Programmers?"
- "You NEED This AI Tool NOW!"
- "This New Model Changes EVERYTHING!"
- "AI Will Replace You in 30 Days!"
- "The Secret AI Nobody Wants You to Know About!"

The channel may discuss major developments, but the presentation must prioritize **technical substance over fear, excitement, or sensationalism**.

---

### 4.2 Trend-Chasing Without Educational Value

Do not create a video simply because:

- A topic is trending.
- Everyone else is covering it.
- It is receiving high search volume.
- An AI tool is temporarily popular.
- A social media post is generating attention.

The question is:

> **Does this topic deserve a permanent place in the learner's knowledge?**

If not, it probably does not belong in the core curriculum.

---

### 4.3 "10 Tools You Need" Content Without Depth

Avoid generic tool-list videos whose primary purpose is showcasing products.

Examples:

- "10 AI Tools Every Student Must Use"
- "15 ChatGPT Alternatives"
- "20 AI Websites You Didn't Know About"

A tool may be covered when it is technically significant or when it supports a meaningful project, but the channel should not become an AI-tool directory.

---

### 4.4 Black-Box Tutorials Presented as Deep Understanding

Avoid presenting:

```text
pip install library
      ↓
copy code
      ↓
run model
      ↓
"Now you understand AI"
```

Using abstractions is valuable in real engineering, but educational content should make clear what the abstraction is hiding when that underlying mechanism is relevant to the lesson.

---

### 4.5 Unsupported Technical Claims

Avoid repeating claims simply because they are common online.

Examples of claims requiring scrutiny:

- Absolute statements about AI capabilities.
- Unqualified performance comparisons.
- "Best model" claims without defined criteria.
- Claims about speed, accuracy, or efficiency without evidence.
- Simplifications that become technically false.

When a claim matters, verify it.

---

### 4.6 Fake Expertise

The channel will not:

- Pretend to have experience that the creator does not have.
- Present speculation as established fact.
- Hide uncertainty to sound authoritative.
- Claim research-level expertise without doing the research.
- Manufacture confidence around concepts that have not been sufficiently understood.

Credibility is built through **transparent reasoning and consistent accuracy**, not through an exaggerated persona.

---

### 4.7 Algorithm-First Content Planning

The channel does not begin with:

> "What will get views?"

It begins with:

> "What should a serious learner understand next?"

Analytics are used to improve:

- Titles.
- Thumbnails.
- Retention.
- Presentation.
- Discoverability.
- Audience understanding.

Analytics do **not** determine the fundamental intellectual direction of the curriculum.

---

### 4.8 Random Topic Publishing

A video should have a defined relationship with the channel's curriculum.

Before production, ask:

1. What series does this belong to?
2. What module does it belong to?
3. What prerequisites does it require?
4. What previous concept does it build upon?
5. What future concept does it prepare the learner for?

If these questions cannot be answered, the topic should be reconsidered.

---

## Final Mission

> **Build a trustworthy, structured, long-lasting Computer Science and AI educational library that helps BSCS students and serious self-learners move from intuition to theory, mathematics, implementation, experimentation, and limitations—while remaining technically honest and resistant to short-term hype.**

### Operating Principle

```text
LEARN DEEPLY
     ↓
RESEARCH
     ↓
VERIFY
     ↓
IMPLEMENT
     ↓
EXPERIMENT
     ↓
EXPLAIN
     ↓
PUBLISH
     ↓
IMPROVE
```

**The goal is not to publish the most videos.**

**The goal is to build the most coherent and trustworthy body of educational work possible as an individual creator.**
